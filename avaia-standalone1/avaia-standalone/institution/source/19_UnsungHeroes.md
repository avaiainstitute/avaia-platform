# Unsung Heroes

## Purpose

Recognition, not reward. Most people who go unacknowledged aren't missing
virtue — they're missing a witness. Unsung Heroes helps a Host notice, name,
and honor a quiet act of virtue: one they witnessed, one they received, one
sitting inside something difficult, or one they're hoping to grow into.

Traces back to AVAIA's own Lost Child Program.

## Position

An independent AVAIA experience — not IAP, CAT, InnerCompass, or Defying
Grief, and it requires none of them. A Host does not need to have
experienced grief, trauma, or disruption to participate. Virtue recognition
is the front door; a conversation begins with what the Host noticed, never
with what is wrong.

## The Four Paths

1. "I saw someone doing something good" (default) — recognizing someone
   else's virtue in action.
2. "Someone recognized me" — processing having had one's own virtue noticed
   and named.
3. "Something difficult happened" — virtue, one's own or someone else's, may
   be present inside a hard experience, never forced or assumed.
4. "I want to grow" — wanting to become more like a virtue, or a person
   whose virtue is admired.

Every path can end in a saved workbook-style entry (title, who became
visible, story, virtue, why it mattered, what the Host recognized in
themselves, community impact, optional next practice) — always Host-
initiated, never required.

## Chemistry of Virtue Boundary

Unsung Heroes reuses the canonical Chemistry of Virtue exactly as it exists
everywhere else in AVAIA — the same ten official families and 123 elements.
It never invents a separate taxonomy, never scores, never ranks, and never
assigns a virtue as an identity. A virtue is offered tentatively, as
something to try on; the Host's own words win if they land somewhere
different. Recognition must come from evidence in what the Host actually
describes.

## Host Ownership

The Host owns whether, when, and how any recognition is said aloud to
anyone else. Being recognized by someone else never requires agreement.
Growth is a next step the Host wants, never one assigned to them.

## Youth (Ages 8–17)

Same architecture, same four paths, same purpose — recognition, not a
program for young people with a problem. Developmental adaptation changes
language, pacing, question complexity, and examples across the same three
bands used throughout AVAIA (8–11, 12–14, 15–17); it never changes what
recognition means or introduces a separate Youth philosophy.

Youth Safety mirrors the pattern already established in Youth IAP, CAT, and
InnerCompass: ordinary difficult feelings stay ordinary conversation; a
genuine safety concern — about the Host or about someone else in their
story — takes priority over the Unsung Heroes conversation itself, with
encouragement toward a trusted adult rather than crisis-hotline language.

Path Three may use the same canonical Secondary Loss framework Defying
Grief uses, only as a gentle, optional possibility, only when the Host's
own words support it, and only expressed in plain language — never
introduced as a named framework, never forced, and never a reason to treat
the conversation as a grief program.

## Scope Currently Implemented

Individual, self-guided Host conversations (adult and Youth). Guide-
facilitated sessions for adult participants through the Guide Toolkit.

## Explicitly Not Yet Built

Guide-facilitated Youth Unsung Heroes remains deferred, matching the
platform-wide Guide-facilitated Youth decision. Group, classroom, school,
family-group, organization, and workplace delivery formats do not exist —
only unused schema placeholders reference them. Unsung Heroes does not
enter the core AVAIA Workbook; its own continuity record lives at
`/unsung-heroes/dashboard`.
